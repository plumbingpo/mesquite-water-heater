How Dallas Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and test the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard to protect the heat exchanger.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The rating plate on the tank usually encodes the manufacture date, and that date tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
