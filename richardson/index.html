How Richardson Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot under the tank, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Richardson

Long before a tank floods a Richardson garage, it usually shows warning signs. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, moisture around the base, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Left alone, they end in a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. A dripping T&P valve deserves special attention: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
