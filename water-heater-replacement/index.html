How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Age is the first clue. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. Then look at the symptoms. Water leaking from the tank, rust showing up only in the hot water, and heavy sediment noise all say the tank is close to finished. A thermocouple, an element, or a relief valve can be swapped on almost any heater; a rusted-out tank cannot, and plenty of households in Mesquite, TX tend to learn that the hard way, with a wet floor.

Replacing a water heater is a chance to get the next one right. Mesquite Water Heater sizes the new unit to your household, not to whatever was printed on the old tank. With a tank, the number that matters is the first-hour rating, which measures the hot water delivered in a busy hour, more than the gallon count. With tankless, it is flow rate in gallons per minute at the temperature rise your water needs in winter. We walk you through the real trade-offs between gas and electric, tank and tankless, and standard and heat pump models, in plain language and without pressure, and we check whether your gas line, venting, and electrical service can support the choice before you commit.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a rushed one look nothing alike. Our technicians remove and haul away the old tank, install the new heater with new connectors and a proper shutoff, and leak-test every gas and water joint. Venting is sized and sloped to spec, a sediment trap goes on the gas line, and the relief valve gets a discharge pipe that ends where it should. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and the permit is handled when your jurisdiction needs one. A standard tank swap is usually done in a day. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Visible corrosion around the pipe connections or burner area
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • A water heater that is more than about ten years old
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Hot water running out faster every month
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

A water heater replacement touches more systems than most people expect. On the gas side there is the supply line, the sediment trap, the shutoff, and a vent that has to draft properly so exhaust never backs up into the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. A correctly rated relief valve has to be piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. Sizing is done by first-hour rating or flow rate. Fuel, venting, and electrical capacity are verified, and whatever expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs get added. We leak-test every joint, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and haul the old heater off. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
